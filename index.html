Recording ideas was never the hard part.

Your phone already has a free recorder. The trouble is what happens after: hundreds of files called New Recording 87, a notes app full of half lines, and nobody ever opens them again.

Most songs don't disappear because they're bad. They disappear because you were tired that day. The Iceberg is built for the part after the recording: getting back to what you made.

Something that didn't click at the time can lead to something good later when you stumble across it.

How it works

  1. 1

    Catch it

    One tap on the widget and you're recording. Or type a line: the draft saves with every letter, so a phone call can't take it from you.

  2. 2

    Let it sink

    Every idea becomes a light in a 3D iceberg. New ones float at the tip; older ones drift slowly deeper. Nothing expires, nothing nags you.

  3. 3

    Get it back

    Tap Break the Ice and a forgotten idea rises to the surface, with the reasons it was picked. Open it, ask for another, or put it back for two weeks.

Resurfacing

The part Voice Memos doesn't do.

Break the Ice picks one older idea and tells you, in plain words, why it picked that one. No mystery, no feed, no algorithm guessing your taste. Just the ideas that have been quiet the longest, and the ones you never finished.

What makes an idea come back

  • It hasn't come up lately
  • How long ago you caught it
  • How long since you last opened it
  • It's still unfinished
  • It isn't connected to anything yet
  • You starred it once
  • It has words or a recording with it

Ideas younger than a week never come back. Anything that surfaced in the last two days waits its turn. A little chance mixes the top few, so it never feels like a fixed list.

  • Waiting inside the app

    Break the Ice sits right on the iceberg screen, next to where you record. You open the app to catch something new, and an old idea is one tap away.

  • One a day on your home screen

    The optional From deeper down widget shows one forgotten idea each day. You see it when you unlock, and you can ignore it.

  • Zero notifications

    The Iceberg doesn't ask for notification permission at all. Old ideas wait for you; they never buzz in your pocket.

Pocket Studio · Plus

Turn a hum into a song sketch, right there on the bus.

When an old idea comes back and it's good, you don't have to wait until you're home. Pocket Studio is a small, real studio on your phone: tracks, blocks, a mixer and a microphone.

  • Up to 16 tracks of drums, chords, bass and melody, in blocks you drag, stretch and split
  • Type your own chords (Am7, F#m, G/B) or start from a ready progression
  • 15 instruments you can shape, 4 bass sounds, any tempo
  • A mixer with faders, pan, mute, solo and live meters
  • Sing over it: count-in, your words for each part shown big, every take kept
  • Export MIDI and finish it in your DAW

Questions

How does it decide which old idea comes back, and when?

Only when you ask, by tapping Break the Ice, or once a day in the optional widget. There's no schedule pushing ideas at you.

When you ask, every idea gets a simple score on your phone. The biggest parts are time: how long ago you caught it, how long since you opened it, and whether it surfaced recently. Unfinished ideas, ideas not connected to anything, starred ideas and ideas with a recording get a small push. One of the top few is picked, and the screen shows you which of those reasons applied. Ideas younger than a week are left alone, and Not now hides one for two weeks.

Won't it just become another notification I swipe away?

It doesn't send notifications at all. The app never asks for that permission. Old ideas live inside the app, right next to the record button, and in a widget only if you add one.

How do I find the right idea without an AI sorting my recordings?

The same way you'd find something in a good notebook, just faster. Search covers your lyrics, titles, projects, key, BPM, chords and the notes you add to memos. Filter by words, voice or melodies, or by starred ideas. Group lines and hums into a project for one song, and connect ideas to each other. When you want a surprise instead, Break the Ice brings up what's been quiet longest.

What happens if I lose my phone or switch to a new one?

Turn on automatic backups and pick a folder that Google Drive, Dropbox or OneDrive already syncs. Every week The Iceberg writes a backup file there, with your voice memos if you like, and keeps the newest three. Your sync app carries it off the phone. On a new phone, install The Iceberg, choose Restore from a backup, check what's inside, and you're back. You can also save a backup by hand any time.

Why not just use Voice Memos or my notes app?

They're great at recording, and The Iceberg doesn't try to beat them at that. It keeps your lyrics and your hums in one place, lets you attach a recording to the line it belongs to, and does the thing they don't: it brings old ideas back to you. You can even share recordings from your old recorder straight into The Iceberg.

Is capturing really fast enough?

From your home screen, one tap on the widget's record button starts a take. There's also a launcher shortcut (long-press the icon) and a share button from other apps. Nothing asks for a title or folder first. If you get interrupted, the take and your typed draft are kept.

Does it work with no signal, on a plane, in the woods?

Yes, always. The app has no internet access at all, so it works the same everywhere.

Is there any AI inside?

No. Nothing in the app generates or rewrites content, and your recordings are never analysed somewhere else. The one experimental tool, Melody Sketch, turns a hummed memo into notes using plain pitch detection on your phone, and it's off unless you switch it on.

Can I get my songs out again?

Yes. Backups are ordinary ZIP files with your audio inside. Studio songs and melodies export as MIDI for any DAW. Your words are plain text you can select and copy anywhere.

Which phones does it run on? Is there an iPhone version?

Android 8.0 or newer. The Iceberg is Android first; an iPhone version is planned for later. Leave your email and you'll hear when that changes.

How does the beta work?

The beta runs as a closed test on Google Play. Leave the email of the Google account you use on your Android phone. When it opens, you get one email with a link to join and install the app from Play. You can leave the test whenever you like, and your ideas stay on your phone.
